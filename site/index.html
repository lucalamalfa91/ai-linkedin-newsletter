#3 Benedict Evans ★ 7/10

Ways to think about token pricing

July 9, 2026

↳ WHAT'S NEW Benedict Evans published an analysis on token pricing economics — examining how AI model labs currently operate in a supply-constrained market and what equilibrium looks like when capacity catches up to demand. ↳ THE REAL STORY The core argument: model labs are pricing like monopolists right now because GPU supply is genuinely scarce. But that's a temporary condition, not a structural moat. As capacity expands — driven by massive capex from hyperscalers and labs alike — the question becomes whether tokens commoditize the way cloud compute did. If inference becomes undifferentiated infrastructure, margin pressure follows the same path as EC2 pricing over the last decade: relentless downward drift. For developers building cost models into their products today, the bet is that token prices continue falling, which changes the math on what's worth building with AI versus what's too expensive to ship.

Claude's take

The uncomfortable question Evans is circling: what actually differentiates one lab's tokens from another's at scale? If GPT-4o and Claude and Gemini converge on capability — which benchmarks increasingly suggest — then price and latency become the only levers. That's a race to the bottom for labs, but a genuine tailwind for builders. The catch is timing: labs are burning capital at a rate that requires premium pricing to survive long enough to reach that equilibrium. If a lab misprices the transition — too slow to cut, too fast to commoditize — it either bleeds out or gets stranded with underutilized capacity. Developers should watch whether labs start competing on price in the next 12 months as a leading indicator that the supply crunch is actually breaking.
