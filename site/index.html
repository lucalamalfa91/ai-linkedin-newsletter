#1 Latent Space ★ 8/10

OpenAI reports median internal Codex output tokens grew 56x in Research, 32x in Customer Support, 27x in Engineering, and 13x in Legal since November 2025

June 26, 2026

↳ WHAT'S NEW OpenAI's internal Codex usage shows median output token counts grew 56x in Research, 32x in Customer Support, 27x in Engineering, and 13x in Legal since November 2025. ↳ THE REAL STORY These multipliers aren't about more users — they're about longer outputs per session. Median output length is the right metric to watch here: it filters out volume noise and captures how deeply employees are actually leaning on the tool. A 56x jump in Research suggests people aren't asking one-line questions anymore — they're getting back full analyses, code files, or multi-step plans. The Legal number (13x) is the most conservative, which tracks: regulated, liability-sensitive work still gets human-trimmed. Engineering at 27x means developers are routinely pulling back substantial code blocks, not snippets. If these ratios hold externally, the implication for API cost modeling is significant — anyone who budgeted for short completions is underestimating their bill.

Claude's take

The catch is that longer outputs aren't the same as better outputs. A 56x increase in Research token output could mean genuinely richer work — or it could mean more verbose, less-edited AI text that researchers are accepting without scrutiny. OpenAI is reporting this as a positive signal, but they're not reporting acceptance rates, edit rates, or downstream quality metrics. Token volume is easy to measure and easy to spin. The number that would actually matter — how much of that output survived into final work product — isn't in the announcement.

#2 Simon Willison ★ 1/10

datasette-export-database 0.3a2

June 25, 2026

↳ WHAT'S NEW datasette-export-database 0.3a2 fixes a dependency pin in pyproject.toml — datasette==1.0a27 is now datasette>=1.0a27. ↳ THE REAL STORY The previous hard pin meant anyone running a newer Datasette version couldn't install this plugin at all — pip would either refuse or silently pull in a downgraded Datasette. One character change (== to >=) unblocks the plugin for the current Datasette ecosystem without any functional code changes.

Claude's take

The fix is trivial, but the underlying pattern isn't: alpha-stage plugins that pin to exact alpha versions of their host framework create silent compatibility landmines. If you maintain Datasette plugins, audit your pyproject.toml constraints now — an accidental == on a moving alpha target is easy to miss in CI if you only test against one environment.

#3 Bruce Schneier

AI and Liability

June 25, 2026

↳ WHAT'S NEW A German court ruled that Google is legally liable for factual errors appearing in its AI Overviews — treating the AI-generated summaries as Google's own statements, not third-party content. ↳ THE REAL STORY This is the liability gap closing in real time. Until now, AI-generated outputs existed in a convenient legal gray zone where vendors could disclaim responsibility by framing the model as a tool. A German court rejected that framing: if you surface the answer, you own the answer. For developers shipping AI-generated content to end users — summaries, recommendations, medical or legal information — this ruling signals that the 'the model said it, not us' defense has a shelf life. Compliance and legal review for AI output pipelines just became less optional in EU jurisdictions.

Claude's take

The catch is jurisdictional scope — for now. Germany's ruling applies to Google under existing German press and information law, not a new AI-specific statute. But it sets a template other EU courts can follow, and it arrives while the EU AI Act's liability provisions are still being operationalized. The open question for builders: does this accelerate demand for output-level fact-checking and audit trails, or does it simply push high-risk AI summary features out of EU markets entirely? Google has the legal budget to fight this; most startups don't.
