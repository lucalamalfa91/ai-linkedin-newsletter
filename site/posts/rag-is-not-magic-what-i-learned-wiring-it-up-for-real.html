Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)

RAG Is Not Magic: What I Learned Wiring It Up for Real

A field note on what actually breaks when you build RAG for enterprise clients — and what doesn't.

November 20, 2026

A client in Zurich had a SharePoint full of internal policies — HR documents, procurement rules, compliance guidelines. About 4,000 files. They wanted employees to ask questions in plain language and get answers. Classic RAG territory. We scoped it, built a proof of concept in two weeks, and it worked beautifully in the demo. Then we handed it to real users and within three days the feedback was brutal: wrong answers, confident ones, citing documents that were two policy revisions out of date. The retrieval was pulling stale chunks and the model was doing exactly what it was told — synthesizing whatever came back. The problem wasn't the LLM. It was what we fed it.

That experience is the most useful thing I can pass on about Retrieval-Augmented Generation. The idea is straightforward: instead of relying on a model's frozen training knowledge, you retrieve relevant text at query time and include it in the prompt. But the technique lives or dies on retrieval quality, chunking strategy, and metadata hygiene — none of which feel glamorous until they go wrong in front of real users.

run-llama/llama_index — Data framework for building RAG pipelines over private data.

The metadata piece — notice the version field in the Chroma example — is what we added after that Zurich incident. Every chunk now carries a document version and a last-modified date. At query time we filter out anything older than the current policy cycle before the similarity search even runs. It's unglamorous plumbing, but it's the difference between a system users trust and one they quietly stop using.

RAG also gets oversold as a drop-in solution for hallucination. It isn't. If the right document isn't in your corpus, or the chunk boundaries split a critical sentence across two pieces, or your embeddings don't capture domain-specific terminology well, the model will still confabulate — just with a citation attached. The retrieval step narrows the search space; it doesn't guarantee the answer is there. Treat it as a precision tool, not a safety net.

“RAG moves the hard problem from the model to your data pipeline — and most teams don't realize that until users are already complaining.”
