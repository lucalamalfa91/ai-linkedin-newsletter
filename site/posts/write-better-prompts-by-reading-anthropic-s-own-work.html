Write Better Prompts by Reading Anthropic's Own Work

Prompt engineering has real structure — steal from the people who test it most.

September 3, 2026

Most prompt engineering advice is circular. "Be specific." "Give examples." "Use clear instructions." True, but useless without a concrete reference point for what good actually looks like in production. The fastest way I've improved my prompting over the past year isn't reading papers — it's reading working examples written by people who test against real models every day. Anthropic's public system prompts are one source. Their Claude Cookbooks repository is a better one for practitioners.

The pattern that shows up repeatedly in well-engineered prompts: role before task, constraints before examples, and output format specified last. Get that order wrong and you waste tokens on corrections or, worse, you get plausible-looking output that silently misses the intent. The other thing worth noticing in the cookbook examples is what's not there — no padding, no excessive politeness, no hedge phrases like "please try to." Every word is load-bearing. That's the discipline most enterprise prompts lack, and it's exactly where quality degrades at scale when prompts get passed between team members and accumulate noise.
