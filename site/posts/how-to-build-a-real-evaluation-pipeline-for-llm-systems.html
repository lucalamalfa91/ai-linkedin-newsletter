How to Build a Real Evaluation Pipeline for LLM Systems

Skip the vibe checks — here's a concrete process for testing LLM outputs you can actually trust.

August 25, 2026

Most teams I talk to are evaluating their LLM systems the same way: they run a few prompts, it looks reasonable, and they ship. That works until it doesn't — and when it breaks in production, they have no baseline to diff against. A proper eval pipeline isn't complicated, but it does require committing to a specific sequence of steps before you touch a model setting or swap a prompt.

Rules for Building a Test Suite That Actually Holds Up

  1. 1

    Cover failure modes, not happy paths

    Your golden dataset should include adversarial inputs, edge cases, and real user queries that caused problems — not just the clean examples where the model already does well.

  2. 2

    Separate deterministic from model-graded checks

    Use exact-match or regex assertions for anything structural (JSON schema, required fields, citation format). Reserve LLM-as-judge only for subjective quality where no ground truth exists.

  3. 3

    Version your evals alongside your prompts

    If you change a prompt and don't update the eval, your scores are measuring the old behavior. Treat evals as first-class code in the same repo, same PR.

  4. 4

    Set a regression threshold before you run

    Decide in advance what score drop counts as a regression — 2%, 5%, whatever fits your risk tolerance. If you decide after seeing results, you're rationalizing, not evaluating.

openai/evals — Framework and open registry of benchmarks for evaluating LLM systems.

Before You Call Any Eval 'Done'

  1. 1

    Check score distribution, not just the mean

    A 0.85 average faithfulness score can hide a cluster of 0.3s on a specific query type. Look at the tail before declaring success.

  2. 2

    Re-run on a held-out slice monthly

    Model drift and prompt drift are real. A score that was fine in January may have quietly degraded by March with no deployment on your side.

  3. 3

    Document what each metric does and doesn't capture

    Faithfulness doesn't catch hallucinations about things not in the retrieved context. Know the blind spots of every metric you report.
