LLM Observability & Tracing

You Cannot Debug What You Cannot See in Your LLM Pipeline

LLM observability is not monitoring — here's the specific difference that changes how you instrument your code.

October 2, 2026

Most teams I talk to have logging. They write prompts and responses to a database, maybe track latency on a dashboard, and call it done. That is not observability — that is archaeology. When something goes wrong in a multi-step chain, you need to reconstruct the exact sequence of calls, which model version was active, what the intermediate outputs looked like, and where the token budget started bleeding. Flat logs do not give you that. Traces do.

The mental shift is from events to spans. A trace wraps an entire request — from the first user message to the final response — and every LLM call, retrieval step, or tool invocation inside it becomes a child span with its own timing, input, output, and metadata. When a chain produces a wrong answer, you open the trace and walk the tree. You do not grep through lines hoping you correlated the right request ID.

One thing worth saying plainly: instrumentation cost is not zero. Adding tracing to a high-throughput pipeline introduces a small network overhead, and if you are not disciplined about sampling, you will pay for storage you never query. Start by tracing 100% in staging, then decide what sampling rate makes sense in production based on actual volume. The traces you capture in the first week of a new deployment will save you hours the first time something breaks — and something always breaks.
